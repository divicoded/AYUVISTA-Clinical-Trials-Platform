#### Q1: "Are these standards like CDISC and HL7 FHIR actually accepted by Indian regulators like CDSCO, or are they only for the US FDA?"
* **Winning Defense**:
  > *"Sir/Ma'am, both Indian and global standards are converging. The National Health Authority (NHA) under the Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission (ABDM) has explicitly mandated HL7 FHIR as India's national health data interchange standard. Furthermore, while CDSCO accepts standard clinical reports, any Indian Ayurvedic pharmaceutical company seeking global market authorization, US FDA botanical drug approval, or WHO pre-qualification is strictly required to submit electronic datasets in CDISC SDTM and Define-XML format. AYUVISTA ensures that Indian trials are simultaneously compliant at home and audit-ready abroad at zero licensing cost."*

#### Q2: "Why did you choose the WHO-UMC causality assessment system instead of the Naranjo scale?"
* **Winning Defense**:
  > *"The National Pharmacovigilance Programme for ASU & H Drugs, headquartered at AIIA (NPvCC), specifically recommends the WHO-UMC causality methodology. Unlike the Naranjo algorithm, which was engineered for single isolated chemical entities and relies heavily on rechallenge scores, the WHO-UMC scale provides clinical categories (Certain, Probable, Possible, Unlikely, Conditional, Unassessable) that accommodate the polyherbal synergy and multi-target pharmacodynamics of classical Ayurvedic formulations."*

#### Q3: "How does your research citation framework translate into actual code in AYUVISTA?"
* **Winning Defense**:
  > *"Every citation is codified as executable logic in our codebase. For instance, CDSCO Rule 22 is implemented as an automated PostgreSQL/SQLite countdown timer calculating `time_left = 24h - (current_time - event_onset)`. CDISC SDTM is implemented as our Python transformer generating `DM.csv`, `AE.csv`, and `Define-XML 2.0`. And 21 CFR Part 11 ALCOA+ is codified as an append-only audit ledger interceptor with cryptographic before/after state diffs."*
